# The 18 choice types

*A choice type names what you do in the moment, not the subject.*

---

Picking a logo and picking an opening sentence are one type: Pick one. They need different displays, but they ask the same act of you. So each type below applies across every field, and each has its own design. You can [see all 18 mockups at once](/mockups/index.html).

This is a proposal. We found no existing taxonomy of choice types across fields for human work with AI, and no standard for choice-making experiences. A search cannot prove that none exists. See the [prior art and evidence](/patterns/prior-art).

## Was the choice real?

The types say what you did. They do not say whether it counted. A rubber stamp and a hard call can both be a yes. So every moment gets one test:

1. Could you have gone another way?
2. Did you know enough to decide?
3. Did your choice change the work?

Three yeses make a real choice. [Cao, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10738) makes a similar point: clicking and selecting are shallow, and the question is whether your reasons shape the work.

## The types

### Frame

Decide what you want, before options exist.

- [Say what you want](/patterns/say-what-you-want): put your intent in your own words.
- [Set what matters](/patterns/set-what-matters): choose the criteria for good.

### Select

Choose among what exists.

- [Pick one](/patterns/pick-one): choose one from a few.
- [Accept or reject](/patterns/accept-or-reject): say yes or no to each item, one or thousands.
- [Rate](/patterns/rate): score an item on a scale.
- [Rank](/patterns/rank): put items in order of preference.
- [Sort](/patterns/sort): put items into groups or slots.
- [Split](/patterns/split): divide a fixed amount.
- [Set a value](/patterns/set-a-value): choose a number on a range.

### Shape

Change the work.

- [Steer](/patterns/steer): say "more like this" and go again.
- [Mark up](/patterns/mark-up): say what to change, on the exact part.
- [Edit](/patterns/edit): make the change yourself.
- [Combine](/patterns/combine): take parts from several options.
- [Arrange](/patterns/arrange): decide the order and structure.

### Govern

Decide how the deciding goes.

- [Set a rule](/patterns/set-a-rule): decide once, for every future case.
- [Hand off](/patterns/hand-off): give this choice to someone else.
- [Look further](/patterns/look-further): refuse to decide yet, and get more.
- [Stop](/patterns/stop): halt what is running.

## Six properties of every moment

1. **Stakes:** what does it cost if you are wrong, and can you undo it? Bezos asks for both: [consequential and irreversible](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312516530910/d168744dex991.htm).
2. **Scale:** one, a few, or thousands. A backlog is one type at large scale.
3. **Ground:** what makes an answer good. Truth, Forecast, Rule, Value or Taste. One moment can have several. This builds on older work: Herbert Simon split facts from values, and McGrath split tasks with correct answers from tasks with none. We read those through secondary sources.
4. **Medium:** how you judge it: by looking, reading, listening or trying it.
5. **Duration:** this one time, or from now on. [ACP](https://agentclientprotocol.com/protocol/tool-calls) already separates these.
6. **Parties:** you alone, or several people together, like a couple or a doctor and a patient.

The type and these properties select the interface. Pick one, with low stakes, a few options, Taste as its ground, and judged by looking, means pictures side by side.

## Every moment needs the same parts

1. Where things stand.
2. The options, in a form you can judge.
3. The reason for each option.
4. A way to answer outside the options.
5. A record of what you chose and why.
6. It works on a phone. People make most of their choices away from a desk, so the phone is the first design, not an adaptation.
